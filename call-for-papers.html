2nd ESR Workshop · NeurIPS 2026

Call for Papers

We welcome work on spatial understanding, reasoning, and generation for embodied agents and world models — new methods, benchmarks, analyses, and position papers.

Deadline Sep 5, 2026 4–8 pages Double-blind Non-archival

Scope

What we are looking for

Embodied spatial reasoning is the ability to interpret the positions, orientations, physical properties, and temporal dynamics of an agent and its surrounding objects in 3D space.

Work on this topic remains fragmented: embodied agents derive spatial reasoning from multimodal alignment, while world models extract priors from video, with no shared interface for spatial, physical, and temporal modeling. The paper track invites submissions that address this gap — from new methods and benchmarks to analyses and position papers that reframe the problem.

There is a single submission format: 4–8 pages, so both short contributions — position papers and extended abstracts — and full papers are welcome. We uphold high standards of review, with impartial and constructive feedback for every submission.

Topics of interest

  1. 01 Spatial understanding and reasoning in embodied agents
  2. 02 Physically-grounded video world models
  3. 03 Object permanence and spatial memory
  4. 04 Physical consistency and long-horizon temporal coherence
  5. 05 Generative and discriminative 3D modeling
  6. 06 Long-horizon planning and interaction in 3D space

Guidelines

Submission guidelines

  • Format A single submission format, covering short papers, position papers, extended abstracts, and full papers alike.
  • Length Submissions of 4–8 pages are allowed. References and the optional appendix do not count toward the page limit.
  • Template Use the NeurIPS 2026 template, following the official NeurIPS 2026 Call for Papers ↗. Submit a single PDF through OpenReview.
  • Anonymity Review is double-blind. Remove identifying information from the main paper and from all supplementary materials.
  • Supplementary Supplementary material may be submitted through OpenReview, but reviewers are not required to consider material outside the main paper.
  • Code & data Links to code and data are welcome and may be included alongside the PDF. Anonymous repositories are allowed.

Review

Review process

  • Committee An estimated 30–50 submissions will be reviewed by a committee of 10 members, supported by 30–50 reviewers.
  • Reviews Each submission receives at least two reviews, managed through OpenReview.
  • Author response There is no author-response phase and no author–reviewer discussion period. Decisions are made directly from the reviews.

Policies

Publication and submission policies

  • Archival status All accepted papers are non-archival and will not appear in the official NeurIPS proceedings. Authors retain copyright and may submit an extended version to a future archival venue, subject to that venue's policies.
  • Visibility Making an accepted paper publicly visible on OpenReview is optional and left to the authors. Reviews are not made public, and rejected submissions and their reviews remain private.
  • Prior work Submissions should describe unpublished work or work in progress. Work already formally published or accepted at an archival venue is not eligible. Preprints, including arXiv postings, are fine.
  • Concurrent work Concurrent submission to another venue is allowed. Authors are responsible for complying with the other venue's dual-submission and prior-publication policies.

Presentation

After acceptance

  • Posters All accepted papers are invited for poster presentation. A subset of outstanding or particularly discussion-provoking submissions is also selected for oral or spotlight presentation.
  • Attendance At least one author of each accepted submission is expected to register for and present the work at the workshop.
  • Camera-ready A revised version after notification is optional. It should address reviewer feedback where appropriate and follow the same page limits as the original submission.
  • Status The presentation format does not affect publication status — oral and poster papers alike remain non-archival.
